# How kanji are best learned: findings used in v13

These are short notes. Every source is a real, published work with a link, and I checked the kanji- and character-specific ones on the web on Oct 20, 2026.

| Topic | Finding | Source | What the app does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spacing | Practice spread over time beats massed practice. The best gap grows with how long you need to remember. | Cepeda et al. 2006, *Psych. Bulletin* — https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 ; Cepeda et al. 2008, *Psych. Science* — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x | Expanding 1/3/7/14/30-day schedule. A 復習 queue collects the words that are due. |
| Retrieval practice | Recalling something yourself beats re-studying it, especially after a delay. Recall also beats mere recognition. | Roediger & Karpicke 2006, *Psych. Science* — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x ; Dunlosky et al. 2013, *PSPI* — https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266 | Reviews open with a blank "recall" box. You write the kanji from its meaning and reading before any guide appears. |
| Interleaving | Mixing items from different sets helps learners tell similar items apart and keep them longer. | Rohrer & Taylor 2007, *Instr. Science* — https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8 ; Kornell & Bjork 2008, *Psych. Science* — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x | The review queue shuffles due words from all lessons together. |
| Handwriting | Writing characters by hand improves form recognition and form-to-meaning links more than reading alone. Typing helps the sound link instead. | Guan et al. 2011, *J. Educ. Psych.* — https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023730 ; Longcamp et al. 2005 (children, Latin letters), *Acta Psychologica* — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2004.10.019 | All practice stays handwriting with stroke order. |
| Radicals/components | In the study, pointing out a character's radicals when it was first shown worked best. That beat teaching the radicals beforehand, teaching them late, or never mentioning them. | Taft & Chung 1999, *Psychologia* 42(4) — https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/using-radicals-in-teaching-chinese-characters-to-second-language-/ | Each component is shown with its keyword at first sight. The 部首 path orders kanji so that a kanji used as a component comes before the kanji built from it. |
| Component mnemonics | Teaching kanji by component analysis gave better retention than whole-kanji memorising, including at one month, in adult learners. A study of story mnemonics after Heisig measured motivation only, not memory. An older study of imagery mnemonics found no long-term benefit. So the evidence is mixed: component links help, but elaborate imagery is not proven. | JALT Journal 20(2), "Effectiveness of Different Approaches to Kanji Education with Second Language Learners" — https://jalt-publications.org/jj/articles/2769-effectiveness-different-approaches-kanji-education-second-language-learners ; Lin, Kajita & Mase 2007, *JALT CALL J.* 3(1-2) — https://www.castledown.com/journals/jaltcall/article/view/jaltcall.v3n1-2.34/519 ; Wang & Thomas 1992, *Language Learning* — https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-1770.1992.tb01340.x | Short links that name the components, written for this app (links.js). They are not long stories. Freehand boxes hide them, so recall stays real. |
| Words/context | Learners infer word meaning best when they combine the kanji in a word with its context. Kanji words are read more accurately in context than in isolation. | Mori 2003, *Modern Language J.* — https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-4781.00198 ; okurigana/context study, *Japanese Psych. Research* — https://doi.org/10.1111/jpr.12596 | Kanji are practised inside JMdict words, and each word is traced at the end. |
| Frequency | High-frequency vocabulary gives the most coverage for each item learned, so learn it first. | Nation, *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language* (Cambridge UP) — https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139524759 | JLPT N5 comes first. Within a level, the order is components-first. |
| Desirable difficulties / fading | Learning sticks better when practice is effortful but still doable. Worked-example support should be faded as skill grows. | Bjork & Bjork 2011 — https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf ; Renkl & Atkinson 2003, *Educ. Psychologist* — https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3 | First practice gets the full flow. After 1 clean practice, you get a recall box, then a trace with part boxes, then a freehand box. After 2 clean practices in a row, you get the recall box only. Using a hint resets this. |
| Feedback timing | Feedback that comes right away, or after a short delay, beats no feedback. Delayed feedback can help retention. Either way, feedback is what matters. | Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2007, *J. Exp. Psych.: Applied* — https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.13.4.273 | A wrong stroke gets a quiet red fade (no text). After 3 misses, a trace guide appears. Each item ends with a "next review" note. |
| Audio / multimodal | Handwriting strengthens the link to the written form, and a sound-based channel strengthens the link to pronunciation. Combining both gave the best results. | Guan et al. 2011 (above) | Words are spoken aloud, and the reading is always on screen. |
| Session length | Shorter, spaced sessions beat one long session for the same total time. | Bloom & Shuell 1981, *J. Educ. Research* — https://doi.org/10.1080/00220671.1981.10885317 | Review is capped at 12 items. 部首 lessons are 5 kanji. |
